## 1. Overview

This opening session of the 10-session Managerial Communication course begins with the instructor's introduction (communications faculty at IIM Indore, ~10 years' experience, PhD from MICA Ahmedabad in human–machine communication, formerly at SPJIMR and NMIMS Mumbai, corporate trainer/consultant for brands including Royal Enfield), a walk-through of the course map (verbal → non-verbal → workplace writing → routine/neutral messages → negative/bad-news messages → meetings → presentation skills → persuasive communication → two sessions of group presentations), and the four-part evaluation scheme. The class then surfaces student expectations and the instructor sets the course's central philosophy: **think about every communication situation through frameworks**. The substantive teaching of the session covers the five pre-requisites of leadership communication (purpose, audience awareness, clarity, timing, credibility) and then dives deep into **audience analysis**, using two video clips (*Panchayat* Season 2 — the open-defecation announcement; *Band Baaja Baaraat* — the wedding-planner pitch) as contrasting case examples. From the discussion, the instructor builds out the **PAIBOC model** (Purpose, Audience, Information, Benefits, Objections, Context), including demographic vs. psychographic audience analysis, features vs. benefits, anticipating objections, the carrot-and-stick approach, and a preview of ethos/pathos/logos. PAIBOC is declared the spine of the entire course.

### F. Five Prerequisites of Leadership/Managerial Communication (clarify BEFORE speaking)
1. **Purpose** — Why am I communicating?
2. **Audience analysis / audience awareness** — Who am I speaking to? Who am I writing this email to? *(Described as the most important part of any leadership communication.)*
3. **Clarity** — If the thought is not clear in your head, you cannot articulate it, even in writing.
4. **Timing** — When do you do it.
5. **Credibility** —
   - **"Credibility is the slowest of the five to build and the fastest to be spent."**
   - Credibility has three components: **Competence, Character, Consideration**.

### G. Discussion Question Posed
> **"What happens when we communicate without understanding our audience, and what changes when we do?"**

Student answers (collectively the "cost of no audience analysis"):
- Without knowing the audience's level, you can't pitch the content at the right level (too high or too low).
- **No one-size-fits-all methodology** — explaining quantum physics to a high-schooler vs. a postgraduate requires different approaches.
- Overloaded slides pull attention away from the speaker. → Instructor endorses the rule: **no more than six lines/points on a slide** ("that's the ideal way").
- Poor curation of ideas → **lack of engagement** → **poor decision-making**.
- The intended message doesn't reach a mixed-age corporate audience; discussions and decisions stall; **speaker and audience are not on the same page**.

### H. Case Example 1 — *Panchayat* Season 2 (web series clip)
**Context given:** The village Sachiv (secretary) has lived in the village 2+ years and knows the demographics. An **open-defecation inspection** is scheduled for the next morning; villagers must be told *today* (time-sensitive) so the inspection goes smoothly. He makes a public announcement over the loudspeaker in the evening.

**Class observations — why it failed on audience analysis:**
- **Faltering opening:** he visibly hesitates over *how* to address the audience (formal "aap" vs. naming villagers) — "the initial part is very important."
- Starts with **"Good evening"** (English) then switches to Hindi, then uses the English phrase **"open defecation"** — code-switching the villagers don't follow.
- **Wrong timing/receptivity:** villagers were settling in to sleep, preoccupied with mosquitoes/mosquito nets.
- **Topic taboo/comfort mismatch:** villagers are not comfortable discussing the subject openly; the loud public mode didn't resonate.
- **Speaker-centred, not listener-centred:** the message was about what *he* wanted to say, not what the listeners needed to receive.
- **Imposition, not persuasion:** framed as an instruction/standard template + a **fine of ₹500** if caught; the villagers didn't perceive open defecation as a problem at all, so the "problem" he was solving wasn't theirs.
- **No benefit offered.** Suggested fix from class: connect to a sense of belonging — "the fields are like your home; keep your home clean."

**Lesson:** Even when a speaker *knows* the demographics and psychographics, failing to **tailor** the message to them renders the communication ineffective.

**Sub-concept introduced here — Carrot-and-Stick Approach (motivation):**
- **Carrot** = incentive/reward for desired behaviour (positive motivation).
- **Stick** = penalty for wrong behaviour (e.g., the ₹500 fine).
- Clip had **only stick, no carrot**.
- Student counterpoint accepted by instructor: because the inspection was *tomorrow* (high time-sensitivity), the **stick approach was arguably the correct choice** here — but the *delivery/framing* still needed audience-appropriate language.

### I. Case Example 2 — *Band Baaja Baaraat* (film clip)
**Context given:** Ranveer Singh (Bittoo) and Anushka Sharma (Shruti) are wedding planners who have so far done only **small-budget weddings**. They pitch to a **wealthy South Delhi client** couple (and later the family) for a large wedding.

**Observations — why it worked:**
- **Thorough prior research** on the clients — knew the groom from Facebook/college, knew he organised his college festival, knew the family's history.
- **Personalised the pitch** by speaking directly to the clients' interests and identity.
- **Knew the competition** (Chandan Narang / "Royal Classic"/"Maharaja" themes) and positioned against it.
- Converted **features into benefits**: "**one client for 6 months, start to finish, 100% total dedication, no compromise**" — contrasted with competitors who juggle many events.
- **Emotional appeal / trust-building line:** "This is not a *deal* for us, this is a *shaadi* for us."
- **Did not back down under resistance** ("we're already in talks with Chandan Narang," "we have your number, we'll call you") — persisted, offered to "walk and talk," walked them to the car.
- Also noted: the *purpose* differed — in *Panchayat* the purpose was merely to inform; here the purpose was to win a big client and grow the business, which drove the research and the hook.

**Second half of the clip — meeting the families (Objections segment):**
Objections raised by the family:
1. **Lack of experience** (first-timers at a wedding of this scale).
2. **Trust** — can they be relied upon with something this large?
3. **Money/investment** — a large amount is being invested.
4. **Reputation** — elite family; a botched wedding invites social gossip and damages standing.

**How Bittoo resolved them:** Connected to the father's own life story — the father was also a first-timer 44 years ago and was *given* a chance; "I deserve the same chance and I deserve to build my reputation." A **pathos/emotional appeal made possible only by audience research.**

**Nuance raised by a student & instructor's answers:**
- *"Isn't being a first-timer a fatal weakness here?"* → They are **not** entirely first-timers: they have a **track record** of small-budget weddings; they're scaling up.
- *"In reality, wouldn't grooming/appearance have sunk the pitch?"* → Valid; **appearance, accessories, clothing all matter for first impressions** — this will be covered in **Session 2 (non-verbal communication)**. Also, allow some slack because it's a film.

## 3. THE PAIBOC MODEL — Core Framework of the Course

> Whenever you craft a presentation, speech, email, or any written communication, start with PAIBOC and "you will never be at fault." Each step is **sequential** — each depends on the step before it.

**P – A – I – B – O – C = Purpose, Audience, Information, Benefits, Objections, Context**

### P — Purpose
- Ask: **"Why am I communicating / writing this email / presenting?"**
- Must be **crystal clear** — ideally **one aim, one purpose**.
- Have the **end goal in mind and work backwards** (e.g., "I am presenting to senior management for approval" → the deck writes itself toward that goal).

### A — Audience
Two broad steps:
- **Demographic analysis** — age, education, occupation, income level, etc.
- **Psychographic analysis** — needs, values, lifestyles, attitudes.
- Research tools today: social media, LinkedIn, Instagram — *"privacy is a myth"*; it's easy to know whom you're dealing with before a negotiation or deal. **Always have your ear to the ground.**
- *Panchayat*: knew demographics/psychographics but **chose not to tailor** → ineffective.
- *BBB*: thorough demographic + psychographic research → nailed the pitch despite resistance.

### I — Information
- **What content will I provide?** — determined entirely by the audience analysis in Step A.
- Personalise the content to the receiver. *BBB* personalised; *Panchayat* did not (ignored education level → "good evening," "open defecation," visible confusion).
- Information also includes **market/competitor intelligence** — knowing the competitor's offering lets you position your product as superior.

### B — Benefits
- Easy but weak: listing **features**. Effective: tying information to **benefits** — "let the audience see what is in store for them."
- *BBB* benefits: exclusivity (one wedding at a time), 100% dedication for 6 months, emotional investment ("this is a shaadi, not a deal").
- *Panchayat* benefit that was missing: cleanliness of the fields as an extension of the home → could have built a sense of belonging instead of imposing a fine.
- Related motivational lever: **carrot (reward/incentive) vs. stick (penalty)** — choose based on situation and time sensitivity.

### O — Objections
- **Anticipate objections in advance.** If you know your business and yourself, you can predict the queries/objections the audience will raise.
- **The simplest trick: step into the shoes of the audience and think from their perspective.**
- > **"Communication is not about the speaker. It is about the audience always."** This distinguishes a good leader from a poor communicator.
- Worked example (*BBB*): anticipated/encountered objections = inexperience, trust, money invested, family reputation → answered with the father's-own-first-chance story (emotional appeal grounded in research).

### C — Context
- **Communication never takes place in isolation.** Factors surrounding it include:
  - **Sentiments of the audience**
  - **Timing**
  - **The environment in which the communication operates** — including the **political scenario**, organisational climate
  - Whether it is a **crisis communication** or a **normal communication**; what is going on at a larger level
- > **"The message changes when the purpose, audience and context change."**

**Course-wide use:** PAIBOC will be applied thematically throughout — writing emails, negative messages, participating in meetings, drafting persuasive messages, and the final group presentations.

## 4. Additional Concepts Named (preview for later sessions)

- **Ethos, Pathos, Logos** (introduced briefly; full treatment in the **persuasive communication** session):
  - **Pathos** — audience driven by emotion → use **emotional storytelling** to convince (what Bittoo used on the father).
  - **Logos** — audience driven by reason, logic, numbers, statistics → use data/evidence.
  - **Ethos** — credibility of the speaker (links back to competence/character/consideration).
  - **Rule:** Diagnose whether your audience is pathos-inclined or logos-inclined and **tweak the communication accordingly** — this is itself part of audience analysis.
- **The C's of communication** — "consideration" specifically flagged (consideration of receiver, culture, language).
- **Slide rule of thumb:** no more than **six lines/points per slide**; overloaded slides steal attention from the speaker.
- **Multicultural caution:** one email to an entire company will be read differently by different people — different backgrounds, education, geographic regions all affect reception; some language can be rude/offensive or carry different meaning across cultures.
